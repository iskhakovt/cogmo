`design/skills.md` carries a staged plan for skill secret exposure and egress control.

`ctx.secrets.get` returns a secret's plaintext value, gated only by the manifest's own `secrets:` list — the skill declaring its own permissions — and `computeRiskTier` classifies a wasm skill with no effects, allowlisted deps and one or two declared secrets as `auto`, which deploys with nobody reading it. Skill bodies are LLM-authored. Reading a credential is half of an exfiltration; the other half is a route off the machine, which is what a host-mediated `ctx.http` supplies.

Five stages, ordered by what actually bounds the damage. Stage 1 is a `network:` allowlist in the manifest with absence meaning no network, plus a risk rule that secrets and egress together never reach `auto` at any count — the load-bearing control, since exfiltration needs a destination the attacker chose, and an allowlist constrains where a credential can travel even while the skill holds it in plaintext. Stage 2 honours the `binding` fields `SkillSecretSchema` parses but does not act on: `secrets.get` yields `cogmo-secret://<uuid>` and `#httpRequest` substitutes the real value at the `fetch`. Stage 3 pins the connection to the address the SSRF guard vetted. Stage 4 vends short-lived tokens for secrets that support exchange. Stage 5 is the proxy, for the one case the others leave.

The proxy is last, and is recorded as additive to stage 2 rather than an alternative to it — a proxy can only substitute for a placeholder the host already issued, so the `secrets.get` work is a precondition either way. The designs it would copy exist to reach inside a socket the platform does not own, and tier 1 has no socket: Pyodide cannot open one, so `ctx.http` runs `fetch` in the host process and substitution is an assignment before the call rather than a CA, a sidecar and `iptables`. Tier 2 keeps raw `httpx`, and a placeholder sent that way reaches the destination as an opaque UUID and earns a 401 — bound secrets fail closed off the host path, which makes `ctx.http` the route of least resistance instead of a rule needing enforcement. Controlling a remote sandbox's egress is therefore off the critical path, which matters because it is not something we can enforce: `design/sandbox.md` scopes policy to where it can be applied, and on managed backends that is the provider's boundary.

The stage markers reflect that: 1 through 3 are `[confirmed]`, 4 `[proposed]`, 5 `[research]`.

The risk-tier table carries secrets-plus-network as its own `approve` trigger, distinct from the 3-secret breadth threshold — the count answers how broad a skill's permissions are, the pairing answers whether a skill can read a credential and also reach somewhere to send it. `ctx.http` is listed as deferred against stage 1 as its release gate.
