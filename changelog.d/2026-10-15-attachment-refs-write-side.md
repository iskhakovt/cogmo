Append-only step 4b: attachments are normalized at arrival and every new attachment row stores refs, so an image turn's row renders the same bytes on every later request and later turns see the image itself, not a JSON string.

- **Arrival.** `create-user-message` turns each inbound attachment into a ref or a placeholder before inserting the row (`normalize-arrival.ts`). Images, including any non-vision `image/*` document, go through `sharp` 0.35.5: HEIC sniffed and refused, a 20 MB byte guard and a 100-megapixel pixel guard, a loader allowlist (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF), EXIF orientation, a 2000 × 2000 fit, JPEG at quality 85 or PNG where transparent, no metadata, at most 3,750,000 bytes. Each image is stored under a key derived from its original's and preceded by an `Image <n>: <path>` label, the path `generate_image`'s `referenceImage` takes. PDFs are stored as sent, unless headerless or encrypted. Text documents are cut to the adapter's per-document byte cap (100,000 on OpenAI-compatible routes, now `AttachmentLimits.textDocumentBytes`) and to a quarter of the input budget, and the cut text is stored beside the original. Other binary documents, URLs, files over 20 MB and attachments past the turn's route budget become placeholders tagged `attachment_placeholder`, counted in `cogmo.attachments.placeholders{reason}`. An inline attachment is uploaded first, keyed by its digest.
- **Original records.** `attachment_originals` (migration 0072) records each image's decode, so a re-batched original reuses its result. A `decoding` marker commits before the first sharp call; a claim that finds one left by a dead process decodes again, and the third claim records the decode-crash placeholder, so a poison input costs two crashes.
- **Telegram.** An image sent as a file carries its name, and a file over 20 MB becomes a size-only block without a download.
- **Runtime image.** The deploy keeps optional dependencies, so sharp's platform binaries ship; `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` and `sharp.concurrency(1)`. `bootstrapCore` round-trips a 1 × 1 JPEG, so an image without the binary fails at boot. A 100-megapixel PNG with alpha and orientation 6 normalizes in 1–3 s with a peak of 217–599 MB.
- **Tests.** The prompt-caching integration suite drops the image turn's declared exception and is re-recorded; a live test sends 21 normalized 2000-px images to Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 5. The engine-range guard skips platform binaries for operating systems Cogmo doesn't run on.
