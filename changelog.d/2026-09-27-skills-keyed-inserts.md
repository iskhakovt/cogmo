`ensureSkillsCodingRepo` writes the `skills` `coding_repos` row through a new `CodingStore.insertOrRecoverRepo` (`ON CONFLICT (name) DO UPDATE` with a no-op SET, `RETURNING (xmax = 0)`) in place of reading the row and inserting on a miss. Two bootstraps can race: boot against the wizard or `migrate-skills-remote`, or integration forks sharing one database. The loser's read cannot see a row committed after its snapshot, so its plain insert failed with `23505` on `coding_repos_name_unique`, which the transactor does not retry. The keyed insert raises `40001` instead, and the retry lands on the existing row, where a stale `remote_url` is synced as before. `SkillStore.startOrRecoverRun` moves from `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` plus a `FOR UPDATE` re-select onto the same shape, one statement that returns the row from either arm.

`.claude/rules/inngest.md` corrects its reason for that shape. On Postgres 18.6 (`pgvector/pgvector:pg18`), with two connections, a REPEATABLE READ insert that conflicts with a row committed after its snapshot raises `40001` under `DO NOTHING` just as under `DO UPDATE`. The source of this is `ExecCheckTupleVisible` in `nodeModifyTable.c`, and the isolation docs limit `DO NOTHING`'s silent skip to Read Committed. The rule used to say `DO NOTHING` skips the row without an error. That was wrong: `startOrRecoverRun` was never broken, and it moves only for consistency. `DO UPDATE` is still the recommended shape because it is a single statement. The rule now names the real hazard: a plain insert, whose `23505` nothing retries. Comments in the coding, scheduling, summary and pipeline stores, and the design docs, now give the corrected reason.
